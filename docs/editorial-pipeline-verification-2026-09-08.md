## Repeatability Fixes

Collection now precedes evidence preparation, editorial admission, whole-edition
selection, source-hash validation, image validation and static rendering. Product Hunt
and routine promotional stock sources are quarantined before review. Thin excerpts
and inaccessible/paywalled bodies are held instead of expanded into invented reports.

Reviews are content/policy cached. Article extraction caches expire after six hours;
full-audio transcripts are cached with their model identity. Source changes invalidate
the publication match. Fresh stories from the previous paper remain eligible despite
per-source candidate budgets. Older saved podcasts cannot leak into future Today views.

An actual rerun exposed a native concurrent-parser crash. The shared extraction wrapper
now serializes native text extraction while network fetching remains concurrent. A
200-task concurrent extraction stress check and a regression test passed afterward.
See the library's [threading guidance](https://lxml.de/FAQ.html#threading).

AP no longer stops scanning after two seen links and bounds attempted requests,
including failures. Live verification retrieved five articles, including one new item.

## Duplication Analysis

Before the candidate budget, 388 eligible stored rows represent 375 distinct URLs:
13 same-URL rediscoveries (3.4%). This is not a semantic duplicate-recall estimate and
does not include every duplicate already rejected by database insertion. Independent
confirmation counts now use unique publisher hosts, not rediscovered rows.

The final desk additionally rejected duplicate WeChat coverage, duplicate Huawei
launch coverage, an incident analysis overlapping the Dwarkesh interview, and an
essay overlapping selected practitioner material. Exact reasons are in the audit.
A labeled sample would still be needed to quantify missed stories and semantic
deduplication precision/recall; the current counts must not be presented as those metrics.
